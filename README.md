# Market Maker Simulator 
This project implements two strategies for market making. These are a naïve strategy which uses a fixed spread with no skew towards bid or ask prices regardless of inventory risk, and a strategy based on the 2008 Avellaneda-Stoikov paper.
## Summary
A list of prices is generated using a Brownian random walk where the change in price at each step is based on the distribution X~N(mu, sigma/sqrt(totalTime)).  At each step, variables like reservationPrice, bid, and ask are recalculated. The market maker then fills any trades. The probability of a trade for a given quote (bid or ask) existing is (A / totalTime) * math.exp(-liquidity * |quote - price|). This means a trade is exponentially less likely to exist and be filled as the market maker's quote moves further away from the mid-price. If the detailed variable is True, lists consisting information about the market maker's position will be updated and at the end of the individual simulation, graphs made using matplotlib will be given. These graphs include price, bid, ask, reservationPrice (if applicable), and PNL.

## Programs
 - fairComparison.py includes a getPrices() function to generate a list of prices which may be iterated through by the avStrategy() and naiveStrategy() functions which are modified versions of the strategies implemented in avStrategy.py and naiveStrategy2.py respectively in order to handle an existing list of prices. This means the two models work on the same data so their results can be compared. A Monte Carlo simulation is used to find the mean and sample standard deviation of the PNL from both strategies. Alternatively to using the getPrices() function, an existing list of prices may be used. This is useful for comparing how the two strategies perform trading on real world assets.
 - naiveStrategy1.py is the original implementation of the naïve strategy. This strategy uses a fixed spread with no skew towards bid or ask prices regardless of inventory risk. Unlike the other models, the distribution for each step of the Brownian random walk is Y\~N(mu, sigma) instead of X\~N(mu, sigma/sqrt(totalTime))
 - naiveStrategy2.py is the updated implementation of the naïve strategy which uses the same market conditions as avStrategy.py, meaning that the two programs and their corresponding functions in fairComparison.py may be compared. This strategy uses a fixed spread with no skew towards bid or ask prices regardless of inventory risk.
 - avStrategy.py is an implemenation of the Avellaneda-Stoikov strategy which bases bid and ask quotes on a reservation price which is adjusted to reduce inventory risk while maintaining expected PNL. For example, if inventory is large, the market maker will try to sell, and decrease its reservation price (and its bid and ask quotes accordingly) so traders are more likely to buy from the market maker than sell.

## Results
I ran a Monte Carlo simulation with 50000 trials to compare the results of the two models in fairComparison.py, using the parameter values suggested in the Variables table. Std. Dev. is the standard deviation of P&L. The Sharpe-ish Ratio is simply Mean P&L / Std. Dev. My results are consistent with those in the original 2008 Avellaneda-Stoikov paper, where naïve is referred to as "symmetric" and the Avellaneda-Stoikov strategy is referred to as "inventory". The Avellaneda-Stoikov strategy provided a comparable mean P&L to the naïve strategy and reduced the standard deviation by 52%, making it worthwhile to market makers for its reduction in inventory risk.
| Strategy           | Mean P&L | Sample Variance | Std. Dev. | Sharpe-ish Ratio | Mean Fills |
| ------------------ | -------- | --------------- | --------- | ---------------- | ---------- |
| Naïve              | 68.64    | 185.0           | 13.60     | 5.047            | 106.4      |
| Avellaneda-Stoikov | 64.88    | 42.17           | 6.494     | 9.991            | 97.00      |
